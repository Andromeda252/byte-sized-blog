The time has come to book a flight back to Wuhu Island as the latest entry in what Nintendo’s now calling the Intuitive Sports series has finally arrived!

Announced back in the June 2026 Nintendo Direct, Nintendo Switch Sports Resort is the direct sequel to Nintendo Switch Sports from 2022. It expands the sports lineup to 12, the same number as the original Wii Sports Resort, with a mix of returning fan favorites and all new sports, as well as introducing new ways to play, such as the single player Island Crown mode, and free exploration of Wuhu Island by land, sea, and sky. Overall, it appears to greatly flesh out the offerings from the first Switch Sports for what will hopefully be a much more fulfilling package.

This was a game I expressed doubt about when it was first announced, as the first Switch Sports was a game that I had a lot of problems with. I definitely didn’t hate it, as I actually found the gameplay of the sports themselves quite fun, but there were so many elements of the game that compounded together to drag the overall package far below what I was hoping for.

I expressed my specific grievances with the game in [a rambler earlier in the month](https://www.the-byte-sized-blog.com/posts/rambling-switch-sports-resort-impressions), but I also expressed a newfound excitement for the game after its segment in the recent Nintendo Direct. And that enthusiasm has carried to today’s launch, as I’m very much looking forward to jumping in!

I’m not expecting the world from this game or anything, but as long as the gameplay is as fun as it was in the previous game and all the new stuff surrounding the core sports helps create a much more complete feeling game, I should be coming away rather satisfied.

I definitely still have some big questions to answer as I start playing the game, such as, will the new side modes and mini games, like the returning 100 Pin Bowling, offer me incentive to play and replay them? Or are they just here for the sake of being here to make the game look like it has more content than it actually does? (Looking right at you, Mario Tennis Fever)

Will Island Crown offer a genuine challenge that requires me to hone my skills and actually strive to get better at each sport? Or will it be something I can just breeze right through with little to no resistance?

Will the online gameplay loop be compelling enough to keep me coming back? Or will it lose me after a week or two?
Will the leisure sports truly be as fun as they look? Or are there hidden restrictions and limitations that will end up dragging down what is, to me, the game’s most compelling feature?

These are just some of the questions I’ll have as I boot the game for the first time later tonight, but regardless of the answers, I’m sure I’ll at least have some good, clean fun. And with Thanksgiving coming up, hopefully I’ll be able to whip it out for some multiplayer action! I even bit the bullet on a new pair of Joy-Con 2 in anticipation, and now my Switch 1 and 2 are matching!

![Both my old Switch 1 and my Switch 2 are now adorned with matching blue and yellow Joy-Con.](/images/nintendo/switch-2/matching-joy-con.jpg)

It's kind of hard to see because the only colored parts of the Joy-Con 2 are beneath the sticks, but I promise the colors are matching!

Regardless, if any of you are planning a return visit to Wuhu Island, or are looking to travel there for the first time, I hope your trip is fun, enjoyable, and most of all, ***ACTIVE!***
