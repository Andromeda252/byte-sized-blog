Happy Nintendo Music Thursday!

So I've been *really* slacking on the Nintendo Music posts lately. And by that I mean, I just haven't been doing them. But I'm getting back to it starting this week, and this time we've got a good one to talk about.

Likely as a part of the Zelda 40th Anniversary festivities, we finally got one of the last remaining major Zelda soundtracks that's been absent from the service, that being The Legend of Zelda: Majora's Mask. The soundtrack spans 111 tracks, totalling 1 hour and 55 minutes of music, and with this being arguably the most haunting Nintendo soundtrack ever composed, it's the perfect way to kick off October!

Seriously, even Nintendo knows how spooky this soundtrack can be, to the point they even included a... bit of a disclaimer on the notification.

![A screenshot of the mobile push notification sent out by the Nintendo Music app upon the addition of the Majora's Mask soundtrack, including a cheeky disclaimer that reads "Fair warning... Some of them are kind of spooky!"](/images/nintendo/n64/zelda-majoras-mask/tloz-mm-nm-notif.png)

But for real, though. There's something about Majora's Mask's entire off-putting vibe that I'm not sure any other game has managed to replicate, and its legendary soundtrack definitely plays a big part in that. Iconic tracks such as Ikana Valley, Stone Tower Temple, and especially the infamous Final Hours (called 'Last Day' on Nintendo Music) are some of the most haunting, genuinely uncomfortable melodies from any game I can think of. These themes help to shape the unforgettable atmosphere of not just one of the darkest Zelda games, but perhaps one of the darkest games Nintendo has ever made.

Final Hours in particular has always jumped out at me as a particularly memorable piece from the soundtrack. It perfectly encapsulates the despair, hopelessness, and begrudging acceptance that has spread across Termina as the apocalyptic impact of the Moon becomes imminent. What's always haunted me about Majora's Mask more than anything else is how, every time Link travels back in time, while he may be able to escape the Moon's destruction, he simultaneously leaves everyone in that timeline to an inevitable doom. Everyone you meet, all the weird and wonderful characters you meet along your journey, there's nothing you can do until the end of the game, at which point you've restarted the loop countless times. And every time you do, Link has no choice but to leave the citizens of Termina to meet an unstoppable end, over and over again until eventually, Majora is defeated and the Moon's descent stopped.

Final Hours is a song that I feel, manages to encapsulate that feeling of hopelessness and powerlessness just about perfectly, to the point where I'd even consider it one of the greatest video game compositions of all time simply for how well it evokes those emotions.

Whew, sorry for rambling there. I may totally be looking too deep into things, but I'd say that's a testament to the lasting impact that Majora's Mask's uniquely dark premise has resonated with me and so many others. The soundtrack plays a big role in why the game is so memorable, and while Final Hours is undoubtedly the highlight for me, it's a soundtrack filled with tracks that similarly manage to evoke unique feelings matched by few other games out there. And now that it's on Nintendo Music, it's definitely worth a listen-through, whether you've heard the game's music a thousand times over, or are hearing it for the first time. I can't recommend it enough.

All that to say that Majora's Mask is truly a one of a kind game, and I'm very happy to finally have its uniquely haunting yet beautiful soundtrack on Nintendo Music. Fingers crossed it gets the remake treatment after Ocarina of Time!
