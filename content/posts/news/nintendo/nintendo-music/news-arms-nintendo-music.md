Happy Nintendo Music Thursday!

Nintendo Switch Sports Resort ended up skipping out on a special release, leaving this week's slot to be filled by something else. I thought we might get the original Switch Sports to coincide with its sequel's release, but that didn't happen either. Instead we got a game that I can only describe as... "oh yeah, that exists."

That's right, the ARMS soundtrack has finally arrived on Nintendo Music! And yes, you'd be forgiven for forgetting ARMS even existed up until this point, cause frankly, I think Nintendo had as well.

This game is an odd one. It was pushed as a big deal back in 2017, when it launched during the Switch 1's release year. It had an incredibly unique premise, being a motion-controlled fighting game with extendable arms, and clearly had a lot of love put into its world, characters, and overall vibe. Like many other Nintendo IPs, there’s nothing quite like ARMS, and between it being a unique take on a popular genre, having a colorful cast of quirky characters, and Nintendo promising continued post launch support via free updates, it’s pretty clear Nintendo was copying the Splatoon playbook beat for beat in hopes that ARMS could become their next Splatoon.

There’s just one problem: the actual next Splatoon was coming out only a month later. 

Now, that’s probably not the only thing that contributed to ARMS failing to take off, as the game didn’t have the same mechanical depth as Splatoon, was lacking content, and didn’t even have a substantial single player campaign like Splatoon did. But I can speak from experience by saying that, despite being interested in the game, I ended up saving my money and waiting for Splatoon 2 about a month later. I would imagine I wasn’t alone in that.

It’s a shame, because ARMS is a really cool game. The gameplay is pretty fun, the characters are memorable, and the world has a ton of potential for expansion. But, while I don’t believe the game sold poorly, it clearly didn’t meet Nintendo’s expectations, and so, to this day ARMS remains a one game franchise that doesn’t seem like it’s coming back anytime soon.

Heck, I think ARMS underperforming is a big reason why Drag X Drive is so dang dry and forgettable. I see it as basically filling the same role as ARMS did on Switch 1 in terms of showcasing the new controllers, but this time they didn’t bother to inject it with any sort of character or world building whatsoever. It really is one of the most bland Nintendo games in recent memory, and I think the underwhelming performance of ARMS is partially responsible, because why bother putting all that effort into building out a world and unique characters for this quirky, gimmicky controller showcase game if there was no guarantee that effort would even lead anywhere.

Again, it’s a shame. I hope Nintendo gives ARMS another shot eventually with a sequel, as it’s an IP that has a ton of potential, if they would only take the time to properly tap into it.

Until that happens, if it ever does, we can at least enjoy the uniquely distinct soundtrack of this one-of-a-kind fighter on Nintendo Music.
