After taking a month off from doing these due to an overall lack of news and simply running out of time, the Nintendo Monthly Roundup returns for its third installment!

After a very quiet August with little in the way of news and no new game releases, September came out swinging with a pair of Nintendo Directs and the release of the latest Switch 2 blockbuster, Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave, a game which, I as a newcomer to the Fire Emblem series, have been having a blast with!

While most of the news and happenings to come out of September were tied to those Nintendo Directs, there was still plenty of other things going on, between a couple free Switch 2 updates for older games, more Nintendo Classics releases, the continued support of Nintendo Music, and one of the more substantial firmware updates in recent memory.

There’s a lot of ground to cover, so let’s get right into it!

# RELEASES

- ### Pikmin 3 Deluxe Update (September 1st, 2026) - [Read More](https://www.the-byte-sized-blog.com/posts/news-pikmin-3-dx-ns2-update)

- ### Mario Kart 8 Deluxe Update (September 1st, 2026) - [Read More](https://www.the-byte-sized-blog.com/posts/news-mk8d-ns2-update)

The month got off to a fast start with free Switch 2 enhancement updates for Pikmin 3 Deluxe and Mario Kart 8 Deluxe on back to back nights. Technically, I believe the Pikmin 3 update was on the last day of August, but I'm including it here for the sake of being able to talk about both of them.

In short, the Pikmin 3 update, version 1.2.0, bumps the resolution and adds GameShare support for Bingo Battle. Meanwhile, the Mario Kart 8 update was a bit more substantial, as version 4.0.0 also bumps the resolution, but goes much further than that by cutting down on loading times, adding CameraPlay, and expanding splitscreen local multiplayer to support up to 8 players! Y'know, just cause they could.

I've linked my write-ups on both updates above if you'd like to read more, as there's a few more details I didn't cover here. I just wanted to hit on the big points.

- ### NES - Nintendo Classics: Ikari Warriors, RC Pro-Am II, Ninja Gaiden III: The Ancient Ship of Doom (September 1st, 2026) - [Read More](https://www.the-byte-sized-blog.com/posts/news-nintendo-classics-updates-9-1-26)

Nintendo Classics kept rolling in September, with the first of two updates last month somehow managing to scrounge up a trio of NES games that haven't been added yet. This update featured SNK's top-down shooter Ikari Warriors, the sequel to Rare's RC Pro-Am, and the third and final game of the classic Ninja Gaiden trilogy, meaning all three games are now available on the service.

- ### Chef Kawasaki and Hop Star amiibo Released (September 3rd, 2026)

If you happen to have ~$50 burning a hole in your pocket, here's another grossly overpriced Kirby Air Riders amiibo! Look, all I'm saying is that Skylanders Swap Force did a similar gimmick for WAY cheaper 13 years ago.

- ### Mario Kart World Ver. 1.8.0. Update (September 9th, 2026) - [Read More](https://www.the-byte-sized-blog.com/posts/news-mario-kart-world-version-1-8-0)

Mario Kart World's biggest update yet was one of many shadowdrops to come out of the general Nintendo Direct on the 9th. This update added another pair of new routes to Knockout Tour, but more substantially, turned all the SNES tribute courses from Free Roam into actual tracks! A total of 10 SNES tracks were added to the game, playable in VS and Time Trials, bringing the game's total track count up to 40!

- ### GameCube - Nintendo Classics: Star Fox Adventures (September 9th, 2026)

Here's something I didn't get around to covering, as the Nintendo Direct coverage I did took way too long to get out. Another shadowdrop, Star Fox Adventures was the second Nintendo Classics drop from last month, bolstering the still-young GameCube library with one of the console's more peculiar titles (and one I actually happen to have a physical copy of!).

Announced alongside the free update to the new Star Fox remake (more on that later), this seems to be another part of Nintendo's recent push for the Star Fox IP following the new game and Fox's Galaxy Movie appearance earlier in the year. It also comes at a time when rumors of an 'adventure' style Star Fox game being in the works are doing the rounds...

- ### Nintendo Switch and Nintendo Switch 2 Firmware Version 23.0.0 Released (September 10th, 2026)

Here's another big thing I didn't get around to covering due to the Nintendo Direct coverage taking too long. The day after the general Direct, Nintendo dropped a substantial firmware update for Switch and Switch 2, bringing the platforms to Version 23.0.0. The headlining features are support for VRR (which I believe stands for Virtual Refresh Rate) in docked mode, and the addition of music to the console's Mii Maker. Now, I personally have no idea what VRR even is, but it seems like a big deal. I'll have to look into it more. On the other hand, music being added to the Mii Maker is really nice to see. There's three music tracks to choose from, each being from a different previous console's Mii Maker, specifically the Wii, 3DS, and Wii U. That's something that's been fairly requested, so even if I never use the Switch's Mii Maker, I do appreciate the addition! Now if they could just do the same for the eShop, and maybe even the home menu, then we'd be in business.

For more information, check out the full patch notes on [Nintendo's official support website!](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/68526/p/1095/c/188)

- ### ***Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave Released (September 17th, 2026)*** - [Read More](https://www.the-byte-sized-blog.com/posts/news-fire-emblem-fortunes-weave-out-now)

The big tentpole release for September 2026 was the latest entry in the Fire Emblem franchise, and as I've mentioned before, it's serving as my first ever foray into the franchise! I had barely ever touched Fire Emblem prior, but have been wanting to give it a try for a while. So, with the hype surrounding Fortune's Weave building in the lead up to launch, I decided that I'd finally be taking the plunge.

I've only logged somewhere between 25 and 30 hours so far, but I'm absolutely loving it. The story, world, and characters have me hooked, and the mechanical depth and sheer variety in its gameplay are super easy to just get lost in for hours at a time. I'll have more to say in a dedicated impressions post that will come... hopefully before the end of October.

The game seems to have generally landed quite well, reviewing highly, receiving positive word of mouth from everything I've seen, and seeming to get off to a strong start sales wise, though we'll likely have to wait until the next financial report for any concrete numbers. Either way, it's great to see the game generating so much buzz, especially for one of Nintendo's more niche (though still quite popular) franchises.

- ### Mineru's Construct amiibo Released (September 17th, 2026)

The latest in the Tears of the Kingdom line of amiibo figures arrived on the same day as Fortune's Weave, though as self-appointed 'Mr-Hasn't-Finished-TotK-Yet', I can't pretend to know what Mineru's Construct is exactly. I know who Mineru is, she's pretty cool, but what her construct is, I'm gonna need to play the game more to find out. At the pace I'm going, hopefully I'll know by the end of the decade.

- ### Star Fox (2026) Free Update (September 29th, 2026)

Announced in the general Nintendo Direct, a free update for the Switch 2 Star Fox remake arrived at the end of the month. The contents of Version 1.2.0 are mainly focused on the game's surprisingly fun multiplayer mode, adding support for local split-screen multiplayer, as well as doubling the number of stages with 3 new ones. You can also take a local team of 4 players into online battles with a single console, which is a sick feature! Not one I'll get much, if any, use out of, but I'm glad it's there.

The update also seems to fix my biggest gripe with the game by making it more obvious when you are dealing damage to a boss. This is a great change, as not knowing if I was doing anything to a boss was a problem I ran into a few times during my playthrough, specifically with the boss of Solar, and the final Andross fight on the Hard Route. When fighting Andross's true form, I genuinely had no idea if I was doing anything until he died, so if this change applies to that fight, then that fixes by far my biggest problem with an otherwise fantastic game. Love to see that!

For more in-depth details, you can view the full patch notes [here!](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/71644/kw/star%20fox/p/1095)

# ANNOUNCEMENTS AND PRESENTATIONS

- ### Zelda 40th Anniversary Direct 9.8.26 and Nintendo Direct 9.9.26 Announced (September 4th, 2026) - [Read More](https://www.the-byte-sized-blog.com/posts/news-nintendo-directs-sept-8-and-9)

- ### The Legend of Zelda 40th Anniversary Direct 9.8.26 (September 8th, 2026)

- ### Nintendo Direct 9.9.26 (September 9th, 2026) - [Read More](https://www.the-byte-sized-blog.com/posts/news-nintendo-directs-sept-26-roundup)

There's not really much I have to say on these that I haven't already said. I covered what I deemed the biggest announcements and put together a big ol' roundup post that took me all dang month to get out. Whoops.

As for my overall thoughts on the Directs, they've definitely changed a bit in the weeks since they aired. In the aftermath of the general Direct specifically, I declared it 'the best Direct of the Switch 2 era so far' while chatting with a friend. That was probably the pent-up excitement from having to wait all day to watch the Direct talking more than anything else, as I wouldn't say that anymore. I'd say that the Switch 2 Direct and last year's September Direct were probably stronger overall. Though, this one was still solid. Metroid Ravenous and Kirby and the World Beyond were great reveals that left me incredibly satisfied, but there was also Hyrule Warriors: Age of Calamity - Definitive Edition, which is something that really appealed directly to my tastes in particular. Having that game is probably a big part of why I was so high on this Direct, though I totally understand that its announcement likely doesn't do much for many others. My excitement for the game is totally a 'me' thing.

Overall, a solid Direct that's hard-carried by its first-party headliners. Take Kirby and Metroid out and you're left with a solid partner showcase with a couple smaller first party updates, but a much weaker general Direct. Those two games are most certainly doing a lot of the heavy-lifting here.

As for the Zelda Direct, getting Age of Calamity - Definitive Edition in the general Direct made me look at this one a bit more fondly. I think I just went into this one with inflated expectations. Though, I wouldn't say it was the most exciting watch. The movie teaser only gave us a logo and official title, and beyond that, it really was just a blowout of information on the Ocarina of Time remake, which does look incredible.

All in all, these two Directs gave us plenty of news to get excited about, but I wouldn't say they left as big of a mark as I would've hoped for out of the idea of two Directs on back to back days.

# NINTENDO MUSIC

I've been really slacking on my Nintendo Music coverage this month. There hasn't been a lot, though. As was expected, things have *really* slowed down since the rollout of Mario Kart World's soundtrack ended back in August, but MAN I'm really missing those Monday drops. Though, despite only getting 4 drops this month, there were some good ones in here. I'll briefly comment on the ones I didn't cover (all of them except Paper Mario) to make up for my lack of covering them.

- ### [Paper Mario](https://www.the-byte-sized-blog.com/posts/news-paper-mario-64-nintendo-music) (September 3rd, 2026)

- ### [Special Release] The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (2026) (September 8th, 2026)

Dropped after the Zelda 40th Anniversary Direct, the special release for Ocarina of Time sadly only includes a single song, the main theme. I actually haven't listened to this yet, and I really should get around to doing so. I'm just a little disappointed that they didn't include a couple more tracks from the hotly anticipated remake. Perhaps we'll get a second special release closer to release, like Kirby Air Riders did last year, that'll include more tracks. This soundtrack is mostly going to be a known quantity, it's just going to be interesting to see how they go about rearranging one of gaming's most legendary OSTs.

- ### WarioWare: Move It! (September 17th, 2026)

This is an interesting one, marking the second WarioWare game on the service, coming alongside a whopping *576 tracks*. The caveat there being that the vast majority of these tracks are, like, 5-10 seconds long due to being composed for short microgames. Though, apparently Nintendo thought this was good enough to leave us without a new drop for two weeks, something they typically only do after particularly substantial drops (two examples I can think of being Tears of the Kingdom and the dual drop of the original Thousand Year Door and its Switch remake). I don't think that's really warranted for WarioWare since the number of tracks makes it seem more substantial than it actually is.

- ### Star Fox (2026) (September 29th, 2026)

Well, that was fast! Only three months after its launch, the soundtrack for Star Fox 64's Switch 2 remake has landed on Nintendo Music! This drop happened on a Tuesday oddly enough, though this is likely so it could coincide with the release of the free update for the game. This soundtrack is pretty sick, as it sounds incredibly cinematic, almost as if it could be a movie soundtrack. Very fitting, considering that playing Star Fox genuinely made me feel like I was playing a summer blockbuster, a feeling that no other Nintendo game has ever instilled in me. Great game, great soundtrack, very happy to have it on Nintendo Music so soon!

# MISCELLANEOUS NEWS

- ### Creator's Voice - Onimusha: Way of the Sword (September 1st, 2026)

- ### Ask the Developer - Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave (September 15th, 2026)

Wrapping up the Nintendo news for the month, we've got a couple things spotlighting two of September's biggest Switch 2 releases.

Firstly, the latest entry in the [Creator's Voice](https://www.nintendo.com/us/gaming-systems/switch-2/featured-games/creators-voice/?utm_source=HW&utm_medium=soytnoa&utm_campaign=S1001-01&utm_id=S1001-01) video series focused on Capcom's Onimusha: Way of the Sword, which launched for Switch 2 and all other major platforms on September 4th.

Then, a couple days before the release of Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave, a new [Ask the Developer](https://www.nintendo.com/us/whatsnew/ask-the-developer-vol-23-fire-emblem-fortunes-weave-part-1/) interview focused on the game was posted to Nintendo's website. These interviews almost always contain interesting behind-the-scenes tidbits on a game's development, and this one was no different. One of the more intriguing pieces of information is that originally, the game was supposed to have eight different protagonists instead of the final 4. This is something that you can definitely see the remnants of in the final game in multiple ways, so it'll be interesting to see if we may potentially get additional routes for some of these characters that were clearly originally intended to be protagonists as DLC. There's a lot of other interesting things in this interview, such as how the game's musical style was settled on and how the character of Eshmel came to be, so I recommend checking it out for yourself!

# LOOKING AHEAD TO OCTOBER

And with that, September is in the rearview mirror. Looking ahead to October, there's a lot happening that we already know of ahead of the dueling meteor strikes of Ocarina of Time and GTA VI landing in November. It's the calm before the storm... except it's anything but calm, as there's a ton of stuff releasing on Switch and Switch 2.

From Nintendo themselves, Nintendo Switch Sports Resort lands on Switch 2 on October 22nd, and I personally have come around to being very excited about this game. It seems to be addressing all my major issues from the first Switch Sports, a game I still enjoyed well enough despite some glaring criticisms, by expanding the offline offerings and changing how acquiring customization items works. Whether or not the end product will end up satisfying me, I'll have to wait and see. I'm trying to keep my expectations in check after getting burned by Mario Tennis Fever earlier in the year, but Switch Sports Resort seems to be doing all the right things so far. I do anticipate that there's more to learn about the game, specifically regarding its online component, so I expect to see another info drop in the coming weeks ahead of the game's launch.

While that's the only confirmed title from Nintendo coming this month, there are whispers doing the rounds that the much-rumored ports of Pokemon Ruby and Sapphire will land this month, perhaps as soon as this week! Pokemon Home is being updated to support the Switch ports of FireRed and LeafGreen later this week, so there could be something there. We'll know sooner rather than later.
