I am incredibly late to this, whoops. Sorry about that, I'm still figuring out exactly how I want to format these things, so I'll be trying something a little different from last month's monthly roundup. I'll probably keep experimenting with these until I find a format that sticks, but for now, let's get right into it.

Following in the wake of an eventful summer showcase season in June, the month of July would not be outdone, being a highly eventful month in the video game industry... but that's not always a good thing.

I'm sure everyone has heard the news that broke throughout the month, and while I'll be beating a dead horse, if I'm going to be recapping July 2026, I can't completely ignore it.

Yes, Sony is killing physical games in 2028, and I'm not happy about it. HOWEVER, I'm not freaking out over it as many online have. I've been preparing myself for this moment for years, knowing it was an inevitability with the way the industry was trending, but it feels surreal that it's actually happening, and much sooner than I anticipated at that.

Now, while I already don't use my PS5 much anymore, with the Switch 2 starting to get many of the games I would play on that platform, this will certainly make PlayStation games released in 2028 and beyond a tougher sell for me. While I'm not against buying games digitally- I grabbed recent Nintendo releases like Tomodachi Life: Living the Dream and Rhythm Heaven Groove digitally as I wanted those games available at a moments notice- I much prefer buying physical. It's to the point where, if Kingdom Hearts IV, the game I bought a PS5 game in anticipation of for all intents and purposes and still intended to buy there even after the Switch 2 version was announced, doesn't release next year and has a kay-card version on Switch 2, I'll probably just go for that instead. We're still too far out for me to definitively make that decision, that is my current plan.

I don't believe the decision is going to hurt Sony's bottom line as much as some, but with the way the announcement was made and lack of communication that's followed, it's hard to deny that the reputation of the PlayStation brand has taken a massive blow, and that sucks. Don't take it the wrong way, I'm not rooting for Sony's downfall or anything. The PlayStation brand is such an important brand in gaming, and the industry is healthiest when all major players are healthy. That's why this whole situation, as well as other news that broke last month, just overall saddens me.

At the end of the day, gaming will go on, whether physical survives or not. I firmly believe that, in time, everything will adapt and settle down to this all digital future, and things will just be the way they are. I don't love it, and I am saddened by the impending loss of physical games as an option. But, ultimately, gaming is my hobby, and while the whole situation undobutedly sucks, I'm at a phase in my life where I have far more important things to worry about, and would prefer to focus on enjoying gaming right now, rather than fixating on the future that is still a ways away and out of my control.

That's just my two cents, though. It's an important topic that does matter a lot to me, so I wanted to cover it. I'll probably write a more in-depth post covering the whole situation at some point, but for now, I thought it would be appropriate to include my thoughts to kick off the month's recap, even if it isn't directly Nintendo related.

Speaking of the Big N... they put out Rhythm Heaven Groove last month, a game that most major publishers would likely never greenlight these days, and my goodness it represents everything I love about Nintendo in the modern gaming landscape.

So, yeah, while there was a lot of cruddy news that came out in July, there was still plenty of good to be found. For their part, it kind of felt like Nintendo was just weathering the storm this month, quietly putting out two banger titles while fires raged elsewhere in the industry.

(Believe me, I'm trying really hard to choose my words so as to not come off as a raging fanboy (even if I admittedly can be sometimes), but that's just how I see it)

This *IS* supposed to be a Nintendo-focused recap, but I just wanted to briefly weigh in on the most pressing story in gaming from the last month. With that out of the way, though, let's dive in for real.

The way I compiled everything this time around was going through the calendar and News section in the Nintendo Today! app, and singling out anything I thought was noteworthy, as well as adding a couple additional things myself, before splitting everything up into a handful of categories: releases, announcements, Nintendo Music, events, and miscellaneous. In each category, I'll have each point listed in chronological order, with a few comments going into further detail on most items.

I have no idea if this format is going to work well, it could end up working great, or being an unmitigted disaster. I won't know until I try, so, here we go!

# RELEASES

Let's start with all the things that came out last month. In June's roundup, I only focused on software, whether it was new games, demos, or Nintendo Classics releases. All of that is still here, but I've expanded the reach of this category to cover major software updates and hardware/accessories, like controllers and amiibo. Let's start at the very beginning of the month.

- Mario Kart World Ver. 1.7.0 (July 1st, 2026)

July got off to a hot start when Mario Kart World received easily its biggest update yet, adding two new Knockout Tour rallys and promising six more to come in the future. The ability to use stickers in photo mode was also added. The promise of more additional content was just as, if not more important than the new content we did get, finally giving us some form of road map for World, hopefully leading up to some more substatial DLC content.

I wrote a more detailied post on this update earlier in the month, which you can check out [here](https://byte-sized-blog.vercel.app/posts/news-mario-kart-world-version-1-7-0)!

- Kirby Air Riders King Dedede & Tank Star amiibo Released (July 1st, 2026)

This one completely went under my radar until putting this roundup together. Coming out pretty much alongside Rhythm Heaven Groove, the absolutely-too-expensive Kirby Air Riders amiibo line continued its rollout with the King Dedede & Tank Star amiibo, which for some reason costs a staggering $54.99. Yeah... that is entirely too much for an amiibo figure. amiibo in general are highly expensive these days, which is a real shame as collecting them isn't as fun as it used to be at these prices.

- ### Rhythm Heaven Groove Released (July 2nd, 2026)

Speaking of Rhythm Heaven Groove... uh, here it is! I immediately fell in love with this game and its unique style, simple yet satisfying gameplay, and incredible soundtrack (Remix 8 and Soda Hop 2 my beloveds). I loved it so much, that I went back and played Fever and Megamix so I could consume as much of the series as possible, because I was *kind of* obsessed for a couple weeks there. That obsession has since died down, but my love for Groove and the Rhythm Heaven series as a whole certainly has not. I'm working on a full review of Groove that I hope to release soon, so I'll go into more detail there. Until then, Rhythm Heaven Groove is simply excellent, and, like I said above, represents eveything I love about Nintendo.

- GB and GBA Nintendo Classics Update Adds Wario Land, Fortified Zone, Sword of Hope II, and Dr. Mario & Puzzle League (July 7th, 2026)

The lone Nintendo Classics drop in July added one of my most wanted Game Boy games yet to be added in Wario Land, a series I've been wanting to play through, but have been waiting for the first two games to arrive on Nintendo Classics. I'll still have to wait for Wario Land 2, but at least I can get started whenever I want to witht the first game and VB Wario Land (still crazy we got that game and Wario World months before Wario Land 1). I haven't gotten to it yet, but I have tried Dr. Mario & Puzzle League, which features a solid version of Puzzle League and a version of Dr. Mario I didn't really care for.

You can read my full thoughts on this drop [here](https://byte-sized-blog.vercel.app/posts/news-nintendo-classics-updates-7-7-26).

- Yoshi and the Mysterious Book eShop Demo Released (July 8th, 2026)

Probably my biggest surprise of the year so far, I'm glad Mysterious Book got a demo so people can try it out and see what makes it unique and special, because, as much as I liked it, it won't be for everyone.

You can read more [here](https://byte-sized-blog.vercel.app/posts/news-yoshi-mysterious-book-demo).

- Free Galaxy-Inspired Update for Mario Tennis Fever Released (July 15th, 2026)

Honestly, I'm glad this happened for the sole reason as it gave me an excuse to talk about by-far my most disappointing game of the Switch 2 era so far. I'm glad they put out some more content for it, though it does little to solve the issues I had with the game, and it's just kind of weird how long it took to get any post-launch content in Fever.

I went into more detail on my thoughts on the update and the game as a whole, and you can check that out [here](https://byte-sized-blog.vercel.app/posts/news-mario-tennis-fever-galaxy-update).

- ### Splatoon Raiders Released (July 23rd, 2026)

The other heavy hitter of the month, Splatoon Raiders is an excellent single-player spinoff in Nintendo's newest major franchise. In essence, the game takes the Salmon Run mode from Splatoon 2 and 3, and, using the power of Switch 2, cranks it up to 11, sending massive mobs of Salmonids your way all while the game maintains a silky smooth framerate. It's not as visually stunning as Mario Kart World or Donkey Kong Bananza, but it uses the Switch 2's enhanced tech in subtler ways like that to create an experience that likely would've been severly compromised on Switch 1, and I just think that's neat. The eventual Splatoon 4 will likely be a larger technical leap for the series that fully utilizes the Switch 2's power, but for a spinoff built off of Splatoon 3, Raiders is an excellent debut for the franchise on Nintendo's latest console. Like with Groove, I aim to put out a full review of the game at some point soon, so keep an eye out for that!
