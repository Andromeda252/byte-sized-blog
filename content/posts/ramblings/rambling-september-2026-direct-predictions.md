It's that time of year again!

As September rapidly approaches, Nintendo fans such as myself have begun the annual ritual of discussing whne we're likely to see a new Nintendo Direct. While nothing is completely confirmed, it certainly does feel like a matter of when versus if. Since the start of the Switch era, there has been a major general September Direct every year except for two, 2020 and 2024, both having pretty good reasons for skipping out. We didn't get a single general Direct in 2020 due to a change in how Nintendo approached news during an uncertain period, and there was no general Direct in September of 2024 simply because there really wasn't much left to announce until the Switch 2 news cycle ramped up. And in both cases, we still got something, with the Super Mario Bros. 35th Anniversary Direct in 2020, and an Indie World + Partner Showcase combo at the tailend of August in 2024, so basically a September Direct.

When you consider that as well as the fact that we still know next to nothing about the Ocarina of Time remake, which is supposed to release in the next 4 months, plus the thin lineup of currently announced 2027 titles, and it becomes pretty clear that a big Direct is almost certainly right around the corner.

So, I wanted to talk about this hypothetical Direct, both regarding what I expect to be there and what I hope to see. I'll be splitting this up into three categories:

- Predictions: Things that I feel fairly confident could be in this Direct.
- Hopes: Things I don't anticipate being there, but are still somewhat realistic and I would love to be proven wrong.
- Pipe Dreams: Things I just generally really want that *probably* aren't gonna happen.

I think this set up covers a wide spectrum of topics ranging from things that are pretty much a lock to things that are highly unlikely but I really want anyway.

Without any further ado, let's jump into some of my predictions.

# Predictions

- ## Ocarina of Time Remake Gameplay and Release Date Revealed, Launching in the First Half of November

Well DUH.

Let's start with a prediction that's about as likely as the Sun coming up tomorrow. Ocarina of Time remake is Nintendo's big blockbuster this holiday, so there's absolutely no chance that they don't show it off in great detail.

I see this Direct being formatted similarly to last year's September Direct, which was essentially a 20 minute Mario 40th Anniversary Direct and a 40 minute general Direct merged into one. I think we'll start off everything for Zelda's 40th Anniversary, perhaps cold opening with a trailer for OOT Remake that properly shows off the game before Eiji Aonuma goes into more detail.

As for the release date, I'm thinking the first half of November makes a lot of sense. Nintendo of all companies probably doesn't need to worry about GTAVI, and since that window has largely been vacated by games trying to get as far away from GTA as possible, it opens the door for Nintendo to waltz in with Ocarina of Time, allowing it to have the first half of November *allllll* to itself before GTA comes out. I originally was going to place it in December, until I realized there's another upcoming Switch 2 exclusive that I feel would be a better fit for December. I'll get to that shortly.

As for what I expect the game to be like? I have *NO* idea. I anticipate it will be more than a simple one-to-one remake in the same vein as Star Fox from earlier this year, but it's hard to predict exactly what the scope will be. Personally, I'd love for it to be the most ambitious remake Nintendo's ever done, setting a new standard for Nintendo remakes. But I just don't know if that's what this is going to be. Plus, I've always found the relatively short time between reveal and release for this remake to feel odd, especially if it were to be as ambitious as I hope.

There's a rumor from a while ago that's stuck with me for whatever reason that this could be a multi-part remake in the same vein as the FFVII remake, with this game we're getting this year covering the Young Link portion of the game. I keep thinking about this for some reason, and it's something I could honestly see. I think it has a lot to do with my mind being unable to comprehend the idea of a potetially massive game we've seen nothing of being just a couple months away.

Hopefully we won't have to wait much longer to learn exactly what this remake is, as it will almost certainly be in this potential Direct.

- ## Zelda Movie Teaser Trailer

This is another one that feels all but certain. Considering the Mario 40th Anniversary segment of last year's September Direct debuted the first teaser for The Super Mario Galaxy Movie, it makes all the sense in the world for the full reveal of Ocarina of Time remake to be accompanied by the first glimpse at the live-action Zelda movie. I don't expect much in this first trailer other than establishing the feel of what this cinematic interpretation of Hyrule is going for. It should also confirm the final name for the movie, whether it be "The Legend of Zelda Movie", or simply just "The Legend of Zelda", which I would personally prefer. I think having 'Movie' in the title of the animated movies works, but for something more mature like Zelda, just the name of the series feels more appropriate.

I've had an idea for a Metroid 6 premise for a while now that I feel would fit this subtitle pretty well.

I'll be discussing spoilers for Dread *and* Fusion here, so feel free to skip this segment if you haven't played them yet and don't want to be spoiled.

- ## Metroid Dread: Nintendo Switch 2 Edition Shadow Drops Digitally, with a Physical Release in Early October

Tying into the Metroid Ravenous prediction, it would make a ton of sense if they dropped a Switch 2 Edition of Dread ahead of Ravenous’s release to encourage people to play Dread before playing Ravenous, as the story of Dread would likely lead straight into Ravenous.

Personally, I would like to replay Dread after playing Fusion now that I have the context of Fusion’s plot, and it would be a great way to prepare for the release of Ravenous in 2027. If Nintendo wants to really push Metroid leading into the 6th 2D entry, a Switch 2 Edition of Dread would be a great way to do so without doing a ton of work.

As for what a Switch 2 Edition of Dread would entail, I don’t see a ton of added content. I see this being a $5 upgrade that really only upgrades the resolution and framerate. Support for 4K 60FPS would be ideal, and I’d also love to see a mode that supports 120FPS. I don’t know why we haven’t seen more Switch 2 Editions add this, as, if Metroid Prime 4, one of the best looking Switch games, could support 120FPS, I don’t see why titles like Mario Wonder and Kirby and the Forgotten Land couldn’t. Considering how fast paced Dread is, a 120FPS mode to make the game feel silky smooth would be sick. Other than that, I see this more so being a vehicle to build hype for Metroid Ravenous by putting out a high quality Metroid title for people to play in the meantime.

- ## Wario Land Returns in Early 2027 with a Samus Returns-esque Reimagining of Wario Land II

This is another prediction stemming from a rumor that’s been doing the rounds.

The leaker who correctly leaked both Rayman Legends Retold and Spyro: A Realm Beyond, both games I’m looking forward to, has claimed Nintendo has a new Wario game in development. I’ve been of the opinion for a while now that Wario Land’s return is inevitable, and I think we could finally see it in this upcoming Direct.

The Wario Land series has seen a lot of support on Nintendo Classics lately, with Wario World, VB Wario Land, and Super Mario Land 3: Wario Land being released on the service in the past year. Curiously, Wario Land II remains absent from the Game Boy library, and combining that information with the rumor of a new Wario game leads me to believe that Wario Land II could be getting a remake as a means of reviving the Wario Land series.

It would fall in line with a strategy Nintendo has had lately when it comes to reviving dormant franchises, starting with a remake of a classic title, and if that generates enough interest, eventually creating a new entry in that franchise. The most prominent example of this is Metroid Dread, which exists because Nintendo commissioned MercurySteam to remake Metroid II. They did such a good job that they gave MercurySteam the keys to make a brand new Metroid game that continued the story of the 2D saga. And with my previous prediction regarding Metroid Ravenous, I believe they will be the home of 2D Metroid for a long time to come. Another potential example is Star Fox, which saw a fantastic remake of Star Fox 64 developed by Velan Studios released earlier this year, and if it’s successful, could lead to a new era of the Star Fox franchise headed by Velan.

So, who would be developing this hypothetical Wario Land II remake? My first thought would be Good Feel, who made the most recent game in the series, Wario Land: Shake It on the Wii. However, they’ve released two games, Yoshi and the Mysterious Book and Princess Peach: Showtime, in the last few years. I can’t see them putting out a third game in such quick succession.

My second thought would be the 2D Mario team, but I highly suspect that they are working on a new Donkey Kong Country as their next project. Perhaps Next Level Games could be working on it, but they’re probably deep in development on Luigi’s Mansion 4 after the success of the third game.

So, my actual guess is that it will be a completely different studio we aren’t thinking of that will be making this potential Wario Land II remake. Perhaps it could be a studio that’s worked with Nintendo before, like how Velan did Mario Kart Live before eventually making the new Star Fox. Perhaps WayForward, who did the Advance Wars 1+2 remake a few years ago? Or it could be someone else entirely! That’s why I’m keeping this vague: it could be anyone!

As for why I specifically am predicting a Wario Land II reimagining, it ties into the lack of Wario Land II on Nintendo Classics. It would’ve made so much sense to add the second game at the same time as the first game back in July, but they didn’t. Perhaps there’s a reason for that.

My usage of the word “reimagining” means that I don’t expect this to be a one-to-one remake, but a game that takes the general design of Wario Land II, but modernizes and expands on it, much like Samus Returns, to the point that it almost feels like a new game.

As for a release date, I definitely see this as a Q1 release. Specifically, I’d place it in mid-February. I think this would make for a great early year game to play while we wait for the bigger stuff to arrive later in 2027.
