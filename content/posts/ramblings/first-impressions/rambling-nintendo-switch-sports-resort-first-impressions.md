Nintendo Switch Sports Resort is a game I’ve had a very interesting relationship with. When it was first revealed, I was indifferent, not expecting it to be much more than its disappointing predecessor.

But, after seeing more of the game during the September 2026 Nintendo Direct, my tune started to change as the things they showed off seemed to genuinely be addressing my problems with the first Switch Sports.

And now that the game is finally in my hands and I’ve had a few good hours to play it, well, I’ve become absolutely obsessed, as not only has it met my expectations, but it’s also exceeded them!

Simply put, I’ve been blown away by just how much the game has been exactly what I wanted out of a Switch Sports sequel. I’ve always found the core gameplay of Switch Sports quite fun, but the content just wasn’t there to back it up. In Switch Sports Resort, they went above and beyond to ensure that there’s actual substance to make the game a much more well-rounded package!

In a recent post on the game, I posed a handful of questions that I hoped to have answered once I started playing the game. I’ve been able to answer most of them at this point, and providing those answers will be the main focus of this post. I’ll save deeper discussions for a full review, but I just wanted to put out my early thoughts on the game and discuss how much I’m enjoying it so far!

So, let’s start with the first of these questions I posed:

***“Will the new side modes and mini games, like the returning 100 Pin Bowling, offer me incentive to play and replay them? Or are they just here for the sake of being here to make the game look like it has more content than it actually does?”***

Yes, they do offer incentive to not only play them, but replay them! Nintendo Switch Sports Resort features in-game Achievements for each sport, much like the original Wii Sports Resort. But, beyond that, many of the side modes, such as the aforementioned 100 Pin Bowling, have their own sets of achievements with various rewards tied to them, such as Spocco Points and various customization options.

Plus, many of these side modes track your high score, so there’s extra incentive to improve your skills and go for better results. These inclusions make these extra modes feel like worthwhile inclusions that I have genuine reason to engage with more than once! I just wish I could play them online. Doing the Skateboarding races (one of my favorite parts of the game so far) online would be an absolute blast!

***“Will Island Crown offer a genuine challenge that requires me to hone my skills and actually strive to get better at each sport? Or will it be something I can just breeze right through with little to no resistance?”***

I haven’t delved too deep into Island Crown yet, but my first impression is positive! It seems like a good way to improve your skills at each sport in the game, as after each match, the Island Champion for the sport you’re playing will interject with a pointer on how to play more effectively. Can’t say I was expecting any form of dialogue in this game, but it’s a neat touch that adds a smidge more character to the mode!

In terms of the actual difficulty curve, I can’t comment too much on that just yet. So far, most of the matches I’ve played have been fairly easy to win, but I’ve only gone beyond the first match in Tennis. I’m currently at either rank 14 or 15 in Tennis, and I have definitely noticed a slight uptick in the CPUs complexity from match to match. Plus, there’s the fact that I got my butt kicked by the first challenger in Bowling, who then proceeded to spam a laughing-crying emoji while I crashed out over my inability to beat a CPU at Wii Sports Bowling. So, if I can expect that kind of genuine challenge from every sport as I progress up the rankings, then I’d say it’s highly likely I’ll come away satisfied.

Also, going back to Tennis for a moment, I’ve noticed that, with every match I play, I feel like I’m getting a better handle on the controls and have more consistently been able to get the ball to go where I want it to go. It’s easy to write off the controls here as mindless waggling (which is probably what it was in Wii Sports, let’s be honest), but they’re so much more intricate than that. There’s a lot that goes into playing optimally, from the timing of your swing, to the direction you swing, and even down to the way you twist your wrist to put spin on the ball or perform different types of shots. It can be difficult to wrap your head around at first, especially since the basic tutorial only teaches you the bare minimum, but by playing multiple matches and rising through the ranks, I feel like I’m slowly but surely understanding it more and more. That’s something the first Switch Sports never gave me an incentive to do, and that alone seems as if it’ll make Island Crown worthwhile if you’re looking to improve your skills!

Oh yeah, there’s also some unlockable customization items you can earn by progressing up each sport’s rankings, giving that much more incentive to partake in the mode. So, yeah, Island Crown seems very promising so far!

***“Will the online gameplay loop be compelling enough to keep me coming back? Or will it lose me after a week or two?”***

I can’t give a definitive answer to this yet, as I’ve only dabbled in the online, but I’ve had a good time playing online so far, and given that the game has given me plenty of incentive to keep playing thus far, I can see myself putting a lot of time into the online, especially once I get access to the Pro Leagues! Given that Island Crown is genuinely helping me improve at the game, I can certainly see myself getting more mileage out of the online here than in the first game.

***“Will the leisure sports truly be as fun as they look? Or are there hidden restrictions and limitations that will end up dragging down what is, to me, the game’s most compelling feature?”***

Based on my experiences so far, the answer to this question is a resounding yes. The leisure sports truly were as fun as I hoped they would be. They are far and away my favorite part of the package, and I’m amazed by just how much there is to them.

This new iteration of Wuhu Island is fully explorable via land, sea, and sky, and I’m blown away by how much detail they’ve packed into it. Nintendo went above and beyond to make the island feel truly alive as you explore, with all kinds of facilities, tourist attractions, and sightseeing spots to discover. Plus, there’s a ton of stuff to collect and missions to complete, and the map is big enough and detailed enough to where casually exploring it is an absolute blast.

All three leisure sports have a handful of sub-modes. They all share the Sightseeing Tour and Free Play modes, with Sightseeing Tour giving you a 5 minute time limit to find as many Spocco Photos at sightseeing spots and major landmarks as you can, while Free Play lets you freely explore with no time limit. There’s also missions to complete, such as collecting a set of coins in a time limit in Skateboarding or popping all the white balloons in a certain region of the map in Prop Plane. The Spocco Photos you collect and missions you complete are tracked in a Sightseeing Log for each sport, meaning each one has a ton for you to do and work towards, making exploration that much more fun and satisfying!

Each leisure sport also has an additional competitive mode unique to that sport. Skateboarding and Power Cruising have races on pre-determined tracks throughout the resort, while Prop Plane has something called ‘Ball Battle’. I’ve only tried the Skateboarding races so far, and it’s a really fun side activity, even solo, as there are preset times for you to beat on each course!

Alright, onto each individual leisure sport.

Power Cruising is the one I’ve put the least amount of time into thus far, only doing the Sightseeing Tour once. I’d probably say it’s my least favorite of the three so far, as there isn’t as much interesting stuff on the water compared to on land. That opinion is subject to change, of course, as I play more. The controls work well, but making precise turns can be incredibly difficult.

Prop Plane is the return of one of the most beloved elements of Wii Sports Resort: the Island Flyover! Having played a ton of Wii Sports Resort at friends' houses growing up (I never owned the game sadly, and still don’t, one of the only Wii series games I’m missing), the Island Flyover was one of my favorite parts of the game, alongside Swordplay, and it feels awesome to finally have a version of that to play in my own household. Flying around is incredibly fun, and the motion controls work very well and are quite intuitive! The only issue I have is that they mapped the balloon buster to the left and right face buttons, which is really awkward to reach when you’re holding the controller the way they want you to. I think it would’ve worked way better, and frankly, been more immersive, if the blaster was instead mapped to the trigger.

And then there’s Skateboarding, which I’m confident in saying is the best part of the entire game. It’s the only sport in the game to utilize mouse controls, and it’s probably one of the best implementations of the gimmick we’ve seen so far. Sliding the Joy-Con 2 to kick, leaning to turn, lifting it up to jump, and shaking it to do tricks all feel incredibly satisfying, especially once you get some speed and are blitzing through the resort on your board. It’s also the most compelling from an exploration standpoint in my opinion, as it lets you get up close and personal with the incredibly detail-packed resort. Being on the island itself, you get a really good feel for the sheer scale of the resort compared to when you are flying over it or restricted to the ocean.

And again, it’s an incredibly cool usage of mouse controls, probably my favorite on the console so far. There have been games that use it in ways that make a ton of sense, such as Metroid Prime 4 or Call of Duty using it for precise aiming, or even Fire Emblem: Fortune’s Weave allowing you to navigate menus and the battlefield with mouse controls. But when it’s come to the more gimmicky, novel uses of the mouse controls, I’ve found everything we’ve seen so far designed around the gimmick to be incredibly shallow. The mouse minigames in Switch 2 Welcome Tour, the Carnival Coaster in Jamboree TV, Bananza's DK Artist mode, and the entirety of Drag x Drive, all have been very one-note with little in the way of substance. Switch Sports Resort’s Skateboarding bucks that trend by not only being a fun and novel usage of mouse controls, but also offering genuine substance and replay value! Once again, it is absolutely my favorite part of Switch Sports Resort, and even after I’ve completed everything else, I can totally see myself continuing to come back to just mindlessly explore the island while performing sick tricks.

And just like that, that’s all my pre-release questions about Switch Sports Resort answered, and so far, all of them have had satisfying answers!

In conclusion, while I haven’t played a ton due to being busy over the weekend, the handful of hours I have played of Nintendo Switch Sports Resort so far have left me nothing short of incredibly impressed. It feels like Nintendo genuinely took all the feedback from the first Switch Sports to heart, and focused on improving the areas where the prior game fell short in order to craft what I find to be one of the biggest surprises of the Switch 2 generation so far.

Perhaps my opinions will change as I play more, and perhaps there will be elements I heaped praise on that I end up souring on with more time as the initial excitement of release dies down. It’s hard to say yet exactly how my final full thoughts in the game will shake out once the dust has settled, but for the time being, I’m loving Nintendo Switch Sports Resort, and cannot *WAIT* to sink more time into it!
