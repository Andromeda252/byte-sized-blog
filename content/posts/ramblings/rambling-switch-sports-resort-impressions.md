I've mentioned in the past how Nintendo Switch Sports Resort was a game I was very skeptical about. I enjoyed the first Switch Sports well enough, the motion controls were solid and the gameplay was actually pretty fun. I can't say I didn't enjoy hopping into it every now and then.

However, despite having some fun with it, it was a game that I ultimately had a plethora of problems with. Firstly, there was the complete lack of any form of substantial offline content. No side modes, no rising through the ranks, nothing. There really wasn't much of a reason to even play offline unless you were playing local with friends, which is obviously a big selling point of the game, but for someone like me who doesn't get to play local multiplayer often, it doesn't really do anything for me. This was an area where even the original Wii Sports had Switch Sports beat, offering rankings for each sport for you to rise through and mini games for each sport that offered medals for earning high scores. None of that was present in Switch Sports.

It gets worse, though. Once you go online, which was the game's primary focus, you'll run into the game's poorly executed progression loop. You can unlock a ranked mode for each sport by playing enough, which is a perfectly fine system that functions as you'd expect, my real issue comes from how they want you to unlock customization options for your Sportsmate avatar. You start out with a bare-bones selection of options to customize your avatar, and you have to play online to unlock more. The method? Earn points by performing well online in the various sports to fill a meter, and once that meter is filled, you get to choose one of a handful of currently available item sets to *RANDOMLY* obtain an item from. Once you have collected every item in a set, you get the headlining item from that set, oftentimes a full outfit or new body type.

I don't think it's hard to see why I *HATE* this system. But if it's not clear, well, it makes you essentially have to grind to get anything you want. And while the gameplay is fun enough, as soon as you ask me to grind for cosmetics and don't even allow me to choose what I want, that's when I lose interest. Unlocking cosmetics in Switch Sports feels like work, and while I'd occasionally come back if there was something I *really* wanted (like the bird body type), this system singlehandedly killed my desire to regularly return to the game, even if I enjoyed it.

Combine that with the staggering lack of content outside of online, and a somewhat weak sports lineup that includes three very similar ball-over-a-net sports (Tennis, Badminton, Volleyball), and it all adds up to a game that I desperately wanted to like, but came away from incredibly disappointed.

That's why I couldn't immediately get excited upon the announcement of Switch Sports Resort back in June.

Despite the admittedly pretty cool decision to return to Wuhu Island after all this time, the initial reveal did little to quell my worries that it would be more of the same. The sports lineup, despite seeing some exciting returning selections like Boxing, Archery, and Prop Plane, as well as intriguing newcomers like Thumb Wrestling and Skateboarding, still left me feeling a little underwhelmed, as many of the sports returning from Switch Sports looked identical to how they did before, and the inclusion of Volleyball alongside Tennis and Table Tennis created the same 'three very similar ball-over-a-net sports' issue the first game had. Plus, I found the exclusion of Chambara/Swordfighting questionable, as that was a big deal in the original Wii Sports Resort, and would've made all the sense in the world in this game.

So yeah, I've been rambling a bit, but hopefully I got across my mixed feelings towards Switch Sports, and why I felt that Switch Sports Resort had a lot to prove ahead of its release later this month.

Welp, dare I say it may have actually proved itself to me.

Last month's general Nintendo Direct featured a segment on Switch Sports Resort that felt tailor-made to address my biggest concerns towards the game stemming from my disappointments with the prior title.

For one, it actually seems to have decently substantial single-player content. Now, it's not like I suddenly expect this game to be the deepest single-player experience ever, but there does seem to be a decent amount of stuff to sink my teeth into when playing offline, compared to the... uh, well, *nothing* that Switch Sports had.

Perhaps what I'm most excited about are the three Leisure Sports that allow you to explore this updated iteration of Wuhu Island in three different ways: by land via Skateboarding, by sea via Power Cruising, and by air via Prop Plane. And it seems that each of these sports will have collectables hidden around the island for you to find, such as Hibiscus Flowers in Prop Plane, or Spocco Photos in Skateboarding (whose descriptions are read out by a text-to-speech voice sounding *very similar* to Rhythm Heaven Groove's Lil' Miss Reeds. I just wanted to mention that cause I think it's funny).

On top of that, offline rankings return with the new Island Crown mode, which allows you to rise through the rankings against increasingly difficult opponents in the 9 non-leisure sports. Eventually, you'll be able to take on the best-of-the-best, including the return of the legendary Mii Champion Matt from Wii Sports. The fact Nintendo made a point of showcasing Matt in the Direct is pretty wild, and tells me that the developers *must* be aware of his iconic meme status in the Nintendo community.

![Matt returns in Nintendo Switch Sports Resort as an Island Legend you can challenge in the new Island Crown single-player mode.](/images/nintendo/switch-2/nintendo-switch-sports-resort/nssr-matt-boxing.avif)

That leads me to another thing I appreciate about the game from what we've seen so far, that being the far greater presence that Miis have compared to the first Switch Sports. In that game, Miis felt like an afterthought, only included to satiate people that preferred to play as their Mii. When you played as a Mii in Switch Sports, it felt like you were just playing as a Sportsmate wearing a Mii mask.

In Resort, they seem to be much more naturally integrated, with footage showing Miis and Sportsmates both congregating in the background during gameplay. The Mii's also just look much more like, well, Miis this time, and a glimpse we get at the character customization screen reveals the availability of standard Mii bodies as selectable outfits. Plus, the simple fact that they're bringing Matt, an iconic Mii character, back speaks volumes about their heightened importance in Resort.

Speaking of character customization, my other massive pain point with Switch Sports beyond single-player/offline content, that seems to be addressed as well! The Direct segment explicitly states that "you can unlock a variety of customizations in the Item Center", which sounds a whole lot like an in-game shop to me. And, sure enough, going to the game's Nintendo store page, it states that you can earn 'Spocco Points' from playing online AND offline that can be spent on new customization options. In other words, it sounds like exactly what I wanted them to do!

We don't have all the details yet, so it could still end up being rotating sets of items, but it does sound to me like you'll have far more direct control over choosing what customization options you want, which is really all I was asking for.

And it doesn't even end there, as the end of the segment shows off what appears to be a bunch of mini-games based on the various sports, including the return of 100-Pin Bowling from Wii Sports! So, not only is the game seemingly addressing my biggest issues with the prior entry by greatly expanding the offline and single-player offerings, as well as overhauling the customization unlock method, but there seems to be even more beyond that with stuff like these mini-games, which hopefully will incentivize high-score chasing and/or be playable online.

Speaking of the online, that's one aspect of the game that we don't really know much about just yet. The website states that "details about how many people can play together online will be provided in the future," which suggests to me that they could still be sitting on some kind of big reveal for this game regarding online play. The last time I recall a statement like this was for Super Mario Bros. Wonder, before they went on to reveal the incredibly unique way in which that game handled online. So, perhaps they're keeping some kind of unexpected online feature underwraps that allows a lot of people to play together in some unique way. It's hard to say, but that's the impression I'm getting from that statement.

Regardless of if it ends up meaning anything, we should get those last few details on Switch Sports Resort sometime rather soon, as the game is out in just two weeks! We can probably expect an Overview Trailer to debut rather soon that will fill in those last few details before launch.

All that said, I'm suddenly actually *really* excited for Nintendo Switch Sports Resort. It genuinely appears to be addressing all my biggest problems with the prior game and then some, and just looks like it'll be a much more complete feeling package at launch, something I've noticed Nintendo doing more lately. It feels like they're moving away from the 'free update' model that dominated, and oftentimes negatively impacted, their casual games during the Switch era. I mean, just look at Mario Tennis Fever. As disappointed as I was by that game, it was pretty fully featured at launch, with an impressive character roster and a bunch of modes (even if I found most of the modes to be as shallow as a puddle). And there's only been one update to the game, and it came in July, 5 months after the game's February launch!

I'm getting a similar impression from Switch Sports Resort, in that it looks like a game that'll be fully featured on day one and won't need to rely on post-launch content to flesh it out. Now, as much as my excitement for the game has spiked, I'm also trying to keep my expectations in check, as the last time I got my hopes up for a Nintendo sports game that seemed to be doing a lot more than its disappointing predecessors, well, I ended up getting burned by the aforementioned Mario Tennis Fever. Though, Switch Sports Resort will have far more gameplay variety than Fever, so I can't imagine there's much I need to worry about.

In conclusion, Nintendo Switch Sports Resort is looking like exactly what I wanted out of a sequel to Nintendo Switch Sports. Between more sports, actual single-player content, and an actual in-game economy with hopefully more player choice regarding the items they acquire, it's looking to be a fun casual experience that'll nicely complement the hardcore titles like Fortune's Weave and Ocarina of Time coming out as the year winds down.

Whether or not those expectations will be met, we'll have to wait until the 22nd to find out. But until then, I cannot *WAIT* to get my hands on Nintendo Switch Sports Resort!
