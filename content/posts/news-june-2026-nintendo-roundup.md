The month of June has come to a close, and with it, we close the book on a fairly exciting summer showcase season. I'll admit, this year's Summer Game Fest and everything around it was the closest this time of year has felt to E3 since it's demise, probably in large part thanks to the 4 major showcases all occuring within a week of each other, but also because of the sheer quantity of exciting games announced and showcased.

Just to name a few: Resident Evil Veronica, Persona 6, Rayman Legends Retold, Alien Isolation 2, God of War Laufey (shoutout my homie Frank the Cube), Gears of War E-Day, Spyro A Realm Beyond (heck yes), Kingdom Hearts IV (HECK YES) and Final Fantasy VII Revelation were all either revealed or showcased in the span of a week early in the month. It also certainly helps that many of the biggest titles announced are Nintendo Switch 2 bound, which feels really cool and admittedly kind of surreal. Like, the idea of FFVII Revelation and Kingdom Hearts IV coming day and date to Nintendo kinda blows my mind.

And that's before we even get into Nintendo themselves, who had themselves a decently eventful month of their own. Of course, it was all headlined by the June 2026 Nintendo Direct, which, while it wasn't my favorite Direct, still had some gobsmackers in store (see: the aforementioned Kingdom Hearts IV), and really showcased Nintendo's arguably stronger than ever third party relations.

It was also a month of N64 nostalgia, with a handful of big announcements and releases relating to that era all coinciding within the month of June. Additionally, we're now getting two Nintendo Music drops a week thanks to something that happened this month as well.

There's a lot to cover in this first ever Nintendo Monthly Roundup, so let's dive right into it.

ANNOUNCEMENTS

The June 2026 Nintendo Direct was a bit lighter on first party news than I had hoped, at I least in terms of stuff that really excites me, personally. That being said, there's still a decent amount to dig into from the Direct.

Kicking things off, the Direct saw three new first party games announced:
- Nintendo Switch Sports Resort (Available 10/22/2026, Switch 2 Exclusive)

I am simultaneously surprised and not surprised by this game's existence. I'm not surprised they're doing a Switch Sports sequel, and I'm not even that surprised it's using the same 'Resort' subtitle as Wii Sports' follow up. What does surprise me is the return of Wuhu Island, though it is a welcome surprise.

I do enjoy Switch Sports, but there's not much reason to play it for long. That's something this game will need to address if it wants my attention for more than a week after launch. It's good to see that there's 12 sports total, but the specific lineup does give me slight pause, mainly because like, half of the roster is sports from Switch Sports that look practically unchanged here. Also, the choice to include Tennis and Volleyball confuses me, as I would've much preferred to see Chambara/Swordfighting back, with that being one of THE sports in Wii Sports Resort. Weird that it's not here. Also, some of the new additions, like Thumb Wrestling, as silly as it is, I just don't find the most exciting. Skateboarding looks like a fun use of mouse controls, though, and it's great to see Table Tennis, Archery, and especially the Island Flyover (now called Prop Plane) returning.

I'm approaching this one with cautious optimism. It has to prove to me that it can offer more than it's predecessor did. We shall see in due time. 

Also bring the full body bird skin from Switch Sports back, that was my favorite part of that game.

- Xenoblade Genesis (Available 2027, Switch 2 Exclusive)

I wish this annoucnement did more for me, as I've loved everything I've played of the Xenoblade games. Chronicles 3 in particular had one of the most engaging and fascinating worlds I've ever encountered in a video game, but ultimately, I just don't have the time needed to dedicate to these massive RPGs these days.

Still, it's great to see the series continuing to recieve support. These are such finely crafted RPGs with some of the best stories and characters you'll find in the genre. It does feel strange to see this one drop the 'Chronicles' subtitle, but this game seems to be the start of a new saga for the series, so it makes sense to differentiate it from the other games in that way.

Not sure if I'll be picking this one up when it launches next year, simply cause I almost certainly won't finish it, but still a solid addition to the upcoming release calendar.

- The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time (2026) (Available 2026, Switch 2 Exclusive)

Alright, this is the big one. Sadly, this one got leaked months in advance, robbing us of a legendary surprise. That doesn't take away from how big of a deal this is.

In case you somehow weren't aware, Ocarina of Time is among the best, most beloved, and most impactful video games of all time. I played it for the first time a decade ago on Wii U Virtual Console, and frankly, it blew me away. There's a reason it is consistently referred to as one of, if not THE greatest game of all time.

I've held the belief for a bit now that if any game deserves the 'reimagining' treatment, its Ocarina of Time. And we don't yet know if that's what this is, but I'm holding on to hope that this is more than a simple remake, but a full-blown modern reimagining, as if Ocarina of Time were made today, just like the Final Fantasy VII Remake trilogy and the Resident Evil remakes.

Unfortunately, the trailer was disappointingly light on details, which especially stung since we already basically knew the game existed. All we really got was a glimpse of Young Link, and a 2026 release year. I'm personally thinking that'll be December 2026, to stay out of the way of GTA VI. Nintendo of all companies could probably get away with launching Zelda right next to GTA, but I see them staying out of the way to be safe.

Regardless, I hope and partially expect this game to be a real showcase for the Switch 2's power, with stunning visuals unmatched by any prior Nintendo game. I also expect them to market it right alongside the upcoming live action Zelda movie, which we should probably be hearing from within the next few months believe it or not. Hopefully, we won't have to wait too long to see more.


So, those were the headliners, but there was quite a bit more first party news sprinkled throughout the Direct.

- Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave gets pencilled in for a September 17th, 2026 release date. I'm not a Fire Emblem guy so apologies that I don't have more to say on this.
- Pokemon Pokopia recieves a free update adding underwater traversal, available August 2026, and a paid expansion pass spanning three waves of content, with the first being a new underwater town, also available in August 2026. Wave 2, adding various new features, is out late 2026, and Wave 3, adding another new town, launches sometime in 2027. Really neat to see substantial post-launch support for this game which... I really need to go back to because it's really good and I just haven't played it in a while.
- The DK Challenge comes to Nintendo Switch Online, challenging members to complete various challenges across a handful of classic Donkey Kong games on NES, SNES, and Game Boy, as well as in Donkey Kong Bananza. Basically the closest we've ever gotten to an achievements system. A really neat idea, and I hope this is the first of many events like this to come.
- Xenoblade Chronicles 1, 2 and 3 all recieve Nintendo Switch 2 Editions to be released throughout the remainder of the year. All three games recieve higher quality visuals, as well as extra goodies and modes that vary between games. And yes, the full title of the first game is, in fact, 'Xenoblade Chronicles: Definitive Edition: Nintendo Switch 2 Edition.' Wow I love video game titles. Xenoblade Definitive Edition NS2 Edition is available now, Xenoblade 2 NS2 Edition launches October 1st, 2026, and Xenoblade 3 NS2 Edition launches December 3rd, 2026. Needless to say you're eating good if you're a Xenoblade fan right now.
- Rhythm Heaven Groove kicked off the Direct with an overview, showcasing more of the game ahead of its release early in July.
- Splatoon Raiders was spotlighted in a short segment, going over the basics of the game, but more so serving to announce the Splatoon Raiders Direct that went down later in the month, and the collaboration Splatfest happening in Splatoon 3 in July.
- A demo for the then upcoming Star Fox remake/reboot was made available directly after the presentation.

That should do it for the Direct's first party content, but really, this was a Direct owned by the third parties. Here's a quick run down of some of the biggest third-party announcements:

- The Duskbloods is recieving a Closed Network Test sometime soon. Sadly we didn't really get any new concrete information about the game itself.
- The Kingdom Hearts series is FINALLY coming NATIVELY to Nintendo, with 1.5+2.5 HD Remix getting a native version on Switch 1 as well as Switch 2, making up for one of my biggest personal dissapointments of the Switch era, and 2.8 Final Chapter Prologue and 3 + Remind hitting Nintendo Switch 2 exclusively. A collection of all the games will also be available for $75, compared to the $120 of buying each seperately.
- Kingdom Hearts IV recieved it's first proper new trailer since its announcement 4 years ago, and is coming day and date to Nintendo Switch 2.
- Onimusha: Way of the Sword launches day and date on Switch 2.
- Dragon's Dogma II: Dark Arisen comes to Switch 2.
- Big Walk, the new game from House House (the Untitled Goose Game devs), heads to Switch 2.
- Ninjala is apparantly still a thing and is getting an open world sequel, Ninjala 2.
- Final Fantasy Resonance announced as the first ever HD-2D Final Fantasy game.
- Pikuniku gets a 3D sequel simply titled Pikuniku 2.
- A new Dragon Quest Monsters game, subtitled The Withered World, was announced.
- Deltarune Chapter 5 confirmed for a late June release, officially putting me three chapters behind whoops.
- Metaphor: Re-Fantazio is headed to Switch 2.
- Minecraft finally gets a native Switch 2 version.
- Devil May Cry 5 heads to Switch 2.

Whew. That was a lot to cover. While this Direct overall may not have been the most exciting for me, I can't deny it was packed with content.

It wasn't the only Direct of the month, however, as we also recieved the aforementioned Splatoon Raiders Direct at the tailend of the month, on June 30th. It was a simple 15 minute presentation offering a deeper glimpse into what to expect from the game, which is looking very promising as a big fan of Splatoon's single player modes. I'm not gonna go over everything, as they covered a lot of ground, but if you're curious about the game and haven't watched the presentation yet, I recommend checking it out.

NINTENDO MUSIC

June 2026 was a pretty awesome month for Nintendo Music. It kicked off with perhaps one of the most highly requested soundtracks, that being Mario Kart World, finally arriving. Alongside it came the promise of regular additions of the game's free roam remixes, only containing the Super Mario Kart remixes at first. This ended up resulting in a two-drops-per-week schedule, with new free roam tracks on Monday, and everything else on Thursday. Not only that, but Mario Kart World was one of two Nintendo Switch 2 soundtracks to drop on the service this month, and the World soundtrack came alongside an update that brought soe nice new features, such as Desktop and CarPlay support.

Here's a quick run-down of everything that happened on Nintendo Music this month:

- Mario Kart World Original Songs and Free Roam with Super Mario Kart (June 1st, 2026)
- Version 1.6.0 Released, Adding Voice Control, CarPlay Support, a My Mix Feature, and Tablet Support, Alonside the Release of a Web Version (June 1st, 2026)
- "Nature" Curated Playlist Added (June 4th, 2026)
- Mario Kart World Free Roam with Super Mario Galaxy and Super Mario Galaxy 2 (June 8th, 2026)
- Wii Sports Resort (June 11th, 2026)
- Mario Kart World Free Roam with Super Mario Bros, Super Mario Bros 2, and Super Mario Bros 3 (June 15th, 2026)
- Hyrule Warriors: Age of Imprisonment (June 18th, 2026)
- "Nintendo 64 Selects" Curated Playlist Added (June 22nd, 2026)
- Mario Kart World Free Roam with Super Mario 64, Mario Kart 64, and Yoshi's Story (June 22nd, 2026)
- Animal Crossing New Horizons Happy Home Paradise (June 25th, 2026)
- Mario Kart World Free Roam with Mario Kart DS and Mario Kart Wii (June 29th, 2026)

Definitely one of the busier months in the service's history, perhaps even the busiest.
