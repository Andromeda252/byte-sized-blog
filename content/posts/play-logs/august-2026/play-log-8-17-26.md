# Splatoon Raiders (NS2)
- ### Played on Mon, Tues, Thurs, Fri, Sat and Sun for an approx. total of ~7 hrs 30 mins

As I mentioned, one of the goals I set for myself this week was to complete the post game of Splatoon Raiders, and on Saturday night, I did just that!

Usually I don't intend to go into spoilers for newly released games in these logs, and Raiders is only just over a month old, but... I really want to talk briefly about some specifics, so, scroll past this section if you'd prefer not to be spoiled.

# POST-GAME SPOILERS FOR SPLATOON RAIDERS BELOW

...still here? Alright, here we go.

The primary post-game of Splatoon Raiders has you unlocking and tackling the seemingly endless underground Salmonid den known as the Bottomless Buffet 3 floors at a time, with the difficulty gradually escalating as you get deeper and deeper.

Once you reach the 100th floor, you're faced with what I can only describe as a super boss. It's not the first time Splatoon's done a super difficult post game boss fight, as those of you who have played the Splatoon 2 Octo Expansion might remember the infamous Inner Agent 3 fight, something I was never able to beat. Inner Agent 3 is different from Raiders' super boss in that Agent 3 tested your true mastery of the game's mechanics, whereas Raiders' superboss feels more like an incredibly powerful bullet(ink)-sponge with dangerous attacks that *WILL* kill you if you get careless. It's still very much a test of the skills you've developed throughout the game, but I find it to feel like a different kind of skill-check than what Inner Agent 3 offers, if that makes sense.

That being said, I did really enjoy this fight! I beat it on my second try, mainly because on my first attempt, I almost immediately lost a life due to being stupid, but it was *incredibly* tense, especially towards the end where I was down to my last life. Every time my health drained and put me in danger was harrowing as I tried desperately to get to safety and wait for my shields to regenerate, especially with the threat of having to redo the entire lengthy fight over again constantly looming overhead.

It was honestly exhilarating, and even though it only took two attempts, winning felt incredibly fulfilling!

And the best part? The game doesn't end, as it turns out the Bottomless Buffet is truly bottomless. After beating the boss, a new set of floors are unlocked, and I imagine it goes on for quite a while longer. There's still more loot to find to get to the highest rank, and while the game will likely be less of a priority from here on, I absolutely intend on continuing to return to it now and again to chip away at these bonus floors.

I also just love the fact that there's more game for the simple reason of being able to continue tweaking my build and making myself stronger, as it seems the difficulty level is only going to keep going up. And I welcome the challenge. This is the kind of end-game content I feel like Side Order was desperately missing- being a rougelike, an endless mode of some kind that progressively gets harder the farther you go would've made so much sense and been awesome!

Regardless, Splatoon Raiders has been a great time, and I'm glad there are still reasons for me to keep going back to it!

# Rhythm Heaven Megamix (3DS)
- ### Played on Thurs, Fri and Sat for a total of 2 hrs 16 mins

Megamix once again served the role as my 'wind-down before bed' game, except for Friday where I played it in the middle of the day just cause. I'm currently working my way through the Towers, and am down to the last individual game and remix of the last tower. Now, I'm pretty sure the game will continue after this, as I have yet to encounter games like Lockstep which I'm pretty sure are in Megamix.

I did finally start seeing some new games in the last two towers I tackled after going through a bunch of sequels, including Rat Race and Tap Trial from GBA, Fan Club and Frog Hop from DS, and Working Dough and Ringside from Fever. It feels kinda surreal to play the Fever games on a 3DS after playing Fever, even going back to the games I played first in Megamix, like Air Rally, Flockstep, and Flipper Flop, before playing Fever.

I also got to try out a different kind of 'Challenge Train' objective, the Monster Maw, where you have to keep getting Aces (perfectly timed inputs) to avoid your screen falling into the mouth of a monster. For every Ace you get, you draw further from the monster, but when you aren't getting Aces, you draw closer to its maw, and if it's able to close its mouth on your screen, you lose. It's an incredibly creative way of recontextualizing these rhythm games in a way that feels incredibly tense and high-pressure. I'd go on extended Ace dryspells, heightening the urgency to get some Aces and find some breathing room, and that added pressure could throw me off my rhythm entirely if I wasn't careful. I love it, it's super cool.

Overall, Megamix is a ton of fun, and I certainly plan to continue chipping away at it whenever I get the opportunity.

# Kingdom Hearts: Chain of Memories (GBA SP)
- ### Played on Friday for ~15 mins

Betcha didn't expect this, huh? I do actually have a real GBA SP, which I got as a birthday present a few years ago. I don't use it a ton, but it's a cool novelty to whip it out every now and then, and I've compiled a pretty neat collection of games for it. Some of them, like Pokemon Emerald, Minish Cap, Super Circuit, and a pair of Super Mario Advance games, are reproduction cartridges. I didn't understand the difference between authentic and reproduction GBA cartridges back when I first got the system, but they're still neat to have. I also have a copy of the Mother 3 fan translation on a GBA cartridge, which, while also clearly not official, is really dang cool. If I ever get around to playing the Mother series, it's how I intend to play the third entry, barring an official translation ever happening (ha).

I do have a couple cartridges that I'm pretty sure are completely authentic, such as Namco Museum, and the topic of this segment, Kingdom Hearts: Chain of Memories. And with my Kingdom Hearts obsession back in full force in lieu of the massive KHIV news from last weekend, I thought it would be neat to pop it in and mess around with it for a bit.

Ever since I came into the Kingdom Hearts fandom and learned about this game, it's fascinated me. The fact that, one game into this series, they decided to make a spin-off on a completely different platform with very different gameplay, and have its story be fairly important to understanding what is going on at the start of KHII is just... well, it's one of the most Kingdom Hearts things of all time.

I've only ever played the PS2 remake, Re:Chain of Memories, and I'm not particularly fond of it- probably my least favorite game in the series- but that doesn't mean I'm any less compelled by the original GBA version.

It's a surprisingly ambitious and impressive game given the hardware it's on. For one, the opening cutscene is a full-motion video that looks just like the cutscenes from the original PS2 game, crammed onto the GBA. It's actually kind of surreal to see this kind of footage running on a GBA's screen. As for the gameplay, they adapted the Kingdom Hearts gameplay to the GBA's much smaller number of buttons about as well as they could.

I pretty much only played up until the first save point, but it was enough to give me a taste of what is one of the most unique titles in the Kingdom Hearts franchise. Like I said, everything about this game is just so interesting to me. I imagine that there were a lot of kids out there that played Kingdom Hearts 1 and 2 and being incredibly confused on how Sora ended up in a memory-restoring sleep pod, unaware at the time that there was an entire other game out there that filled in that gap. I will never not find the decision to handle the early story in this way to be one of the decisions of all time.

But, like I said, if there's any franchise I'd expect that from, it's this one, and wouldn't have this crazy, fever dream of a series any other way.

# Donkey Kong Bananza (NS2)
- ### Played on Sunday for ~45 mins

With the Nintendo Classics half of the DK Challenge complete, I only have one challenge left to clear in Donkey Kong Bananza, and it's the one I had been dreading the most since I first saw it: Complete Final Rehearsal with 0 Skills Unlocked.

Final Rehearsal is this game's Grandmaster Galaxy/Champion's Road/Darker Side equivalent, but my first time playing it, I found it really easy. So, with how quickly I was able to clear the Boss Rush with no skills, I went into it Sunday afternoon with high hopes that I could get it done swiftly.

That's when I learned that Final Rehearsal is a completely different beast with no skills unlocked.

I started by playing through it again with all my skills still unlocked to get a feel for everything and refresh myself on each challenge, as it's probably been over a year since I played it. Immediately, I could see the problems that would be posed. Final Rehearsal rightfully feels like it was designed with an entire playthrough's worth of unlocking skills in mind, so taking those skills away would suddenly make the level require some... *creative* solutions.

And once I had returned from resetting my skills and starting doing attempts, it dawned on me that it was going to be even worse than I feared.

The very first segment of the stage is a tunnel of thorns that I've always used Ostrich Bananza's Glide to maneuver through. But since Glide is an unlockable skill, a different approach would be required.

I tried using Elephant Bananza to suck up the thorns and clear a path, but the very limited Bananergy you have access to due to having no upgrades unlocked makes that unviable. So I tried sucking up thorns with Elephant near the starting platform to create a chunk to Turf Surf over the thorns on. Turns out those chunks are worn down by the thorns super quickly and you'll soon be caught in no man's land where the thorns will almost certainly kill you.

Oh yeah, that brings up the other factor that makes this stupidly challenging: you only have three hearts, but in my experience, it only takes two hits to kill you from full health. So the margin for error is *RAZOR* thin. I have gear equipped that passively restores HP while in a Bananza, but it's so slow and the amount of time I can be in a Bananza before having to refuel is so small, that it barely makes a difference.

So, how in the heck are you supposed to get out of the first area? Well, looking at the video example reveals a technique that combines Ostrich Bananza's default Flutter ability with rapidly starting and cancelling a Turf Surf to *LITERALLY HOVER THROUGH THE AIR!* This looks like tech that I'd see in a speedrun, not as an officially Nintendo endorsed method of getting through this challenge's first area.

Even with that, getting a run out of the thorny tunnel is incredibly difficult. Maneuvering in mid-air while spamming the ZL button to rapidly activate/cancel Turf Surf while managing the amount of Flutter you have (I'm pretty sure I ran out of Flutter on one attempt and plummeted into the thorns) as *WELL* as managing your Bananergy so you don't lose your Ostrich Bananza makes for an utterly *INSANE* challenge that I cannot believe *Nintendo themselves actually wants you to do.*

And you know what? I kinda love it. It turns what was a forgettably easy final challenge into one of the most difficult challenges I've taken on in a platformer in some time, and, like many other challenges in the DK Challenge, goes well beyond what the game itself asks you to do for 100% completion. That's the kind of thing that makes me wish Nintendo had a system-wide achievements system like pretty much every other platform these days, and the DK Challenge offering a taste of what kind of unique challenges that could provide has only emboldened that desire.

But y'know what's really crazy? That's just the first section of the level. To even *REACH* the rest of the level, you have to overcome what might be the most difficult challenge I've faced in Bananza by a wide margin.

Now, the rest of the level doesn't seem nearly as bad, and perfectly reasonable to complete without skills unlocked, but because the first section is so ludicrously difficult to get through and clearly was not designed for 0 skills to the point of needing officially endorsed exploits to get through it, there's a tension that hangs over the entire run, as one wrong move, one misstep, could send you all the way back to the beginning and you have to do the first section again. It makes every move feel the need to be incredibly calculated. It's incredibly tense, but also kind of exhilarating.

In my ~45 minutes of attempts, I only escaped the thorny tunnel once, and was making good progress until I panicked during the radiant stone section and died to spikes. It was at that point I decided to shelve it for the night and opt to grind away at the challenge throughout the coming week. I'm on a strict time limit with only a week until the DK Challenge is over, but I firmly believe I am capable of pulling it off. If I can *just* get another run or two out of the thorny tunnel, or start consistently getting past it, I think I've got a good chance. The challenge comes from developing that consistency and getting more runs past the first section than I don't. And if there's anything I've learned from my time speedrunning games like Celeste a few years ago, it's that if you throw yourself at a difficult challenge enough times, eventually, consistency will come.
